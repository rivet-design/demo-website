<ToolRow

In medieval times, the makers of the<br />
time wrote on parchment. Because<br />
parchment was expensive and<br />
scarce, it was hard to try ideas.

Paper became abundant before the<br />
Renaissance. Early designers like<br />
Leonardo now had notebooks<br />
to fill with ideas. Because it got<br />
cheaper to explore a design, more<br />
ideas flourished.

Parchment was the original design<br />
tool—a blank canvas that invited<br />
the owner to pour their ideas into.

</ToolRow>

<ToolRow
  label="Drafting tables"
  heading="Tools kept progressing"
  src="/images/about/drafting-studio.webp"
  alt="Designers working at drafting tables in a mid-century design studio"
>

Drafting tables of the 1960s. The<br />
earliest computer-aided design<br />
programs of the ’70s. The<br />
beginnings of modern design<br />
software with Photoshop, then<br />
Sketch and Figma.

With each new tool, it became<br />
easier to explore, change, and<br />
develop an idea.

</ToolRow>

<ToolRow
  label="Today"
  heading="The canvas is still a canvas"
  src="/images/about/design-vellum.webp"

Yesterday’s and today’s tools are<br />
closer than you might think.<br />
Modern tools like Figma are like<br />
digital parchment. They’re<br />
flexible and expressive, but it’s<br />
dependent on you to fill in.

<p><Accent>They don’t understand you.<br />
They can’t challenge or<br />
augment your thinking.</Accent></p>

</ToolRow>
